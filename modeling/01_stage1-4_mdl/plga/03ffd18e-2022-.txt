contraception: Preparation, characterization and modelling of drug release
Sowmya Sivasankaran , Sriramakamal Jonnalagadda *
Department of Pharmaceutical Sciences, University of the Sciences in Philadelphia, United States




A R T I C L E I N F O                                       A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                   The integration of mechanistic modeling and machine learning facilitates the understanding and engineering of
Spray drying                                                drug release from controlled release systems. Here, we present hybrid models to predict the effect of drug loading
Controlled release                                          on levonorgestrel release from spray-dried poly(L-lactic acid) microparticles. We developed three Monte Carlo
Contraception
                                                            methods that differ in the consideration of polymer’s degradability and crystallinity, to simulate drug release
Injectable
Monte Carlo simulation
                                                            from the matrices using the Python programming language. To build each method, we utilized data from the
Regression modelling                                        characterization of the particles, such as the actual drug content (ranges from 6% to 52%), size (Dv(50) ~ 5 μm),
Mechanistic modelling                                       and polymer crystallinity (ranges from 0% to 15%). We trained each method using drug release data from
Microparticles                                              particles of 4 batches and derived appropriate machine learning models through regression analysis. Results
Machine learning                                            indicate the contribution of drug diffusion and polymer degradation to drug release for particles of lower drug
                                                            content (<20 %w/w). At higher drug loadings, particles encountered a combination of burst and diffusional
                                                            release. We validated the predictive powers of the machine learning models by testing them against experimental
                                                            data. This paper specifically highlights the power of hybrid modeling to engineer drug release for long-term
                                                            contraception.
